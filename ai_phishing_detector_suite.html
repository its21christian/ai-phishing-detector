Anatomy of Phishing & Smishing Attacks

1. Artificial Urgency & Coercion

Attackers manufacture panic to bypass critical thinking ("Immediate Action Required", "Your wallet will be deactivated within 12 hours", "Arrest Warrant Issued"). Legitimate institutions rarely impose hyper-compressed ultimatums.

2. Domain Typosquatting & Punycode

Adversaries register lookalike domains using character swapping (e.g., paypa1.com instead of paypal.com), subdomains masquerading as main sites (paypal.com.verify-billing.xyz), or Cyrillic homoglyphs.

3. Credential Harvesting Links

The call to action directs to simulated login pages designed to harvest Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) tokens, passwords, or recovery seeds through deceptive web forms.

4. Header & Display Spoofing

The display name appears as "Chase Security", but inspecting the raw RFC headers reveals mismatched Envelope Senders (e.g., reply-to: attacker@relay02.ru) and failing SPF/DKIM authentication records.
